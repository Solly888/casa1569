# CASA 1569 — Historical Foundation

## Purpose

This file defines the historical territory behind the CASA 1569 brand.

It is not a marketing licence to overstate Portuguese primacy.

## Central proposition

Portugal was present very early in Europe's direct encounter with Chinese tea culture through sixteenth-century contact with China and the Portuguese maritime world centred partly on Macau.

## Gaspar da Cruz

Portuguese Dominican friar Gaspar da Cruz produced an early Portuguese eyewitness account of China.

His *Tractado* was published in Évora around 1569–1570.

His account includes a description of Chinese hospitality involving a warm drink called `chá`.

For CASA 1569:

> **1569 is a point of departure.**

It is not the founding date of the company.

## Language

Portuguese uses `chá`, reflecting a linguistic route distinct from the `tea / thé / Tee` family common in much of Western Europe.

This is useful brand territory, but linguistic claims should be sourced carefully.

## Macau

Macau became an important node of Portuguese-Asian exchange.

Do not state that Portuguese merchants alone or primarily introduced tea commercially to Europe.

Northern European Dutch and later British tea commerce was commercially decisive.

## Catherine of Braganza

Catherine of Braganza married Charles II in 1662.

Tea was already known in England before her arrival.

Safer formulation:

> Catherine became strongly associated with tea drinking at the English court and helped make the ritual fashionable.

Do not write:

> Catherine introduced tea to England.

## Açores

Tea later took physical root in Portuguese territory, particularly São Miguel in the Azores.

This closes the CASA 1569 narrative elegantly:

China → early Portuguese encounter → chá → cultural routes → Portuguese tea cultivation.

## Brand ethics

The modern story is about:
- exchange;
- hospitality;
- provenance;
- producers;
- landscape;
- craft;
- routes of ideas and products.

It is not about celebrating colonial domination.
