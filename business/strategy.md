# CASA 1569 — Business Strategy

## What are we building?

A digital-native premium house beginning with origin-led tea.

Not:
- a large catalogue marketplace;
- a flavoured-tea novelty store;
- a wellness supplement company.

CASA 1569 competes on:
- provenance;
- cultural authority;
- design;
- producer storytelling;
- curation;
- customer ritual;
- trust.

## Customer proposition

> Exceptional tea from remarkable places, traced to origin.

## Launch thesis

A tightly curated founding collection creates more authority than a broad catalogue.

The initial collection should contain five routes with distinct geography, cup profiles and stories.

## Founding collection

1. São Miguel — Portuguese-grown flagship.
2. Wuyishan/Fujian — historical bridge to the Chinese story of chá.
3. Darjeeling — seasonal prestige.
4. Ceylon Highlands — refined repeatable black tea.
5. Wazuka — premium Japanese green tea.

Assam becomes the first commercial expansion route.

## Commercial model

Preferred early operating model:

**origin producer → bulk sealed tea → Australian import/co-pack → CASA packaging → DTC customer**

Rationale:
- origin remains authentic;
- CASA controls presentation;
- low initial inventory risk;
- one Australian packing workflow;
- easier SKU consistency;
- founder avoids running a packing room.

## Product strategy

Prefer:
- pure Camellia sinensis at launch;
- 5 products;
- approximately 80g premium tins;
- refill pouch later or at launch if economics support it;
- discovery set;
- limited seasonal lots.

Avoid premature:
- herbal blends;
- functional claims;
- dozens of flavoured SKUs;
- complex RTD products.

## Pricing hypothesis

Final prices depend on landed-cost model.

Premium positioning should support:
- healthy DTC gross margin;
- quality packaging;
- sampling;
- acquisition;
- future wholesale.

Do not lock RRP until sample quality, origin cost, freight and co-packing are known.

## Moat

The moat is not simply tea.

It is the accumulated CASA 1569 world:
- trusted sourcing relationships;
- route archive;
- producer stories;
- distinctive design system;
- cultural/historical authority;
- customer collection behaviour;
- editorial content;
- proprietary operating data.
